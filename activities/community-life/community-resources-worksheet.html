Using a Community Resource in Boston

Task 2 of 4 — Community Life Task Class

Part 1: Reading About Community Resources

Look at the resource descriptions below. Answer the questions for each resource using complete sentences when possible.

Resource 1: Boston Public Library — Central Library (Copley Square)
  1. What do you need to bring to get a library card?
  2. Is a library card free?
  3. Name two things you can do at the library besides borrow books.
  4. What are the general hours (weekday vs. weekend)?
  5. Why might a library be a useful resource for someone new to Boston?
Resource 2: Neighborhood Branch Library (e.g. East Boston or Dorchester branch)
  1. How is a branch library different from the Central Library?
  2. What ESOL or adult programs might a branch library offer?
  3. Do you need an appointment, or can you walk in?
  4. Is this location closer to your home or work?
  5. What would you ask a librarian on your first visit?
Resource 3: Community Health Center
  1. What is the difference between a community health center and a hospital emergency room?
  2. Do you need insurance to be seen?
  3. What languages are interpreters usually available for?
  4. How would you make a first appointment?
  5. Why might someone choose a community health center over a hospital?
Resource 4: Adult Education / ESOL Program
  1. What levels of English classes are usually offered?
  2. Is there a waitlist to join a class?
  3. What do you need to bring to register?
  4. Are classes during the day, evening, or both?
  5. What other services (childcare, job training) might be offered alongside classes?
Resource 5: Boston Centers for Youth & Families (Community Center)
  1. What age groups can use a community center?
  2. Name two activities or programs offered.
  3. Is membership free or does it cost money?
  4. How would a parent sign up their child for a program?
  5. Why might this be useful for a family new to a neighborhood?

Part 3: Persona Matching

Read about each person or family. Choose the best resource for them. Be ready to explain your answer.

A. The New Arrival

Ana just moved to Boston last month. She wants to improve her English and meet people, but she works during the day.

Best resource: _______

B. The Parent

Best resource: _______

C. The Uninsured Worker

Best resource: _______

D. The Job Seeker

David wants to practice his computer skills and use the internet to apply for jobs, but doesn't have a computer at home.

Best resource: _______

E. The Beginner Student

Best resource: _______

Part 4: Discussion Questions

Discuss with a partner or small group.

  1. Which resource would be most useful to you personally right now? Why?
  2. Which resource did you not know about before this activity?
  3. What is one thing that would make it hard for someone to use one of these resources (hours, location, language, cost)?
  4. Imagine you are one of the people in Part 3. What is the first question you would ask when you arrive?
  5. What does "walk-in" mean? How is it different from needing an appointment?
  6. If a friend was new to your neighborhood, which resource would you tell them about first? Why?

Part 5: Asking Follow-Up Questions

The 4-Level Question Strategy:

  • Initial: Ask the basic question (What? When? How much?)
  • Clarify: Get specific details ("Does that include...?" "Do I need...?")
  • Dig deeper: Ask about flexibility, exceptions, options
  • Plan ahead: "What if...?" "How would...?" "What would happen if...?"
