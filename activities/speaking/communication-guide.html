Essential Communication Strategies

Important: Native English speakers pause all the time. Pausing is normal. Pausing is okay.

When You Need Time to Think

"One second."
Very common. Very natural.
"Hold on."
People say this every day.
"Wait."
One word is enough.
"Give me a second."
Polite and clear.

When You Are Looking for Word

"Um..." / "Uh..."
Everyone uses these sounds.
"Let me think..."
This shows you are thinking.
"How do I say this..."
You can say this out loud.
"What's the word..."
Very common in English.

When You Need a Repeat

"Sorry?" / "Pardon?"
Polite and very common.
"Can you repeat that?"
Clear and helpful.
"Say that again?"
Friendly and casual.

Checking Details

"Can you spell that?"
For names and addresses.
"Can you write it down?"
Helpful for numbers/dates.
"Slowly, please."
Very normal to ask.

Asking for Meaning

Clarification & Meaning

"Could you explain what [word] means?"
Using modals for politeness.
"I'm not familiar with that term. Could you define it?"
A more professional way to ask.
"Could you give me an example of how to use [word]?"
Asking for context.
"Is there a more natural way to say [phrase]?"
Focusing on native-like fluency.
Remember: Taking time does not mean weak English. It means careful English.

Survival & Life-Skills Scripts

Important: In emergencies, short sentences are best. Simple English is good English.
Calling 911
"I need help."
"My address is [your address]."
"There is a [fire / accident / injury / robbery]."
"Please come."
Poison Control (1-800-222-1222)
"Someone swallowed something."
"Age: [number]"
"How much? [a little / a lot / I don't know]"
"When? [minutes ago]"
Calling Your Landlord
"Hi, this is [your name]."
"Apartment [number]."
"The [heat / toilet / sink] is broken."
"Can you fix it?"
"When can someone come?"
Rent Increase
"I got your letter about the rent increase."
"Can we negotiate?"
"Can you do [lower amount]?"
"I need time to think."
"Can I have until [date]?"
"I can't pay that much."
"This is too expensive."
Calling the Pharmacy
"I need a refill for [medicine name]."
"My Rx number is [number]."
"My birthday is [month / day]."
"When will it be ready?"
Professional Absence (Work/School)
"Hi, this is [your name]. I'm calling to report an absence."
"Unfortunately, I won't be able to come in today due to an illness."
"I've taken care of my immediate [tasks/assignments]."
"I expect to be back by [day / date]."
"I'll be available via email if anything urgent arises. Thank you."
Doctor's Appointment
"I'd like to schedule an appointment with [doctor name]."
"I'm calling for a [check-up / specific problem]."
"Do you have anything available [this week / next month]?"
"Does my insurance cover this visit?"
Returning an Item
"I'd like to return this [item]."
"It didn't [fit / work / meet my needs]."
"I have the receipt right here."
"Can I get a refund to my [card / original payment]?"
Lost Debit/Credit Card
"I'm calling to report a lost card."
"My name is [your name] and my account number is [number]."
"Please freeze my account immediately."
"When can I expect a replacement card in the mail?"
You need: Who you are. What the problem is. What you need.
You do not need: Perfect grammar.
