How it works

1. One partner sees

Other partner looks away.

→

2. Remember

Read or watch—then it’s gone.

→

3. Tell

Say everything you remember.

→

4. Answer

Partner B answers. Then switch roles.

Both get a turn

Switch and do it again.

Whole class

Paraphrase

Same meaning — new words. Your version can be shorter or longer.

Original (mini-summary)

“Lucia felt dizzy and sick at the bus stop in the cold. Her sister took her to urgent care. The nurse checked her; the doctor said she was dehydrated from skipping meals, not seriously ill. She drank water and felt relieved.”

Paraphrase (shorter)

“Lucia got sick at the bus stop, so her sister took her to urgent care. They said she was dehydrated from stress—not a serious illness—and she felt relieved.”

Shorter is fine. Keep the main ideas; you don’t need every detail.

Together: Teacher reads a short summary. You: say the same story in new words—shorter or longer is OK.

Questions — Partner A

How long had Omar been coughing?
For three frustrating days, keeping his wife awake at night.
What was the pharmacist doing when Omar noticed the problem?
The tired-looking pharmacist was checking his ID.
How did Omar feel when he saw the wrong name?
His stomach dropped. He worried about taking the wrong medicine.
Why did Omar switch to Spanish?
To make sure the pharmacist understood. He wanted to communicate clearly about the mistake.
What had Omar done by the time he got home?
He had already texted his wife about the close call and had set multiple alarms.
What might have happened if Omar hadn't noticed?
He could have taken the wrong medicine, which could be dangerous.
