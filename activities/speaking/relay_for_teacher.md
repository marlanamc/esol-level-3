## Round 1: Reading Scripts

### Reading 1 — Lucia's Story: Urgent Care Morning

**Vocab bank:** shivering, dizzy, trembling, embarrassed, dehydrated, fluorescent, gratefully, relieved

**Full Text:**

At 7:10 a.m., Lucia was shivering at the bus stop in the cold morning air when she started feeling dizzy. Her heart was racing. She called her sister with trembling hands. By the time her sister arrived, Lucia had already vomited once on the sidewalk, feeling embarrassed and scared. They rushed to urgent care, where the bright fluorescent lights made her squint. The nurse was gently taking her temperature when Lucia suddenly remembered—she had been so stressed about her job interview that she'd skipped dinner and breakfast. The doctor smiled kindly and said she was dehydrated, not seriously ill. Before they left, Lucia had gratefully drunk two cups of cool water and had eaten salty crackers that settled her stomach. She felt relieved—it wasn't anything serious.

**Comprehension Questions & Answers:**

1. Where was Lucia when she started feeling dizzy?
   - **Answer:** She was shivering at the bus stop in the cold morning air.

2. What had happened before her sister arrived?
   - **Answer:** Lucia had already vomited once on the sidewalk.

3. How did Lucia feel when she vomited?
   - **Answer:** She felt embarrassed and scared.

4. Why do you think Lucia skipped meals?
   - **Answer:** She was stressed about her job interview.

5. What was the nurse doing when Lucia remembered something?
   - **Answer:** The nurse was gently taking her temperature.

6. How did Lucia feel at the end?
   - **Answer:** She felt relieved—it wasn't anything serious.

### Reading 2 — Omar's Story: Pharmacy Mix-Up

**Vocab bank:** frustrating, neighborhood, sharp, stomach dropped, politely, firmly, switching, pale, shaking, close call

**Full Text:**

Omar had been coughing for three frustrating days, keeping his wife awake at night, so he went to his neighborhood pharmacy after his long shift at work. While the tired-looking pharmacist was checking his ID, Omar's sharp eyes noticed the medicine label had another patient's name—Maria Rodriguez, not Omar Hassan. His stomach dropped. What if he had taken the wrong medicine? Before he took anything, he politely but firmly asked for help, switching to Spanish to make sure the pharmacist understood. The pharmacist's face went pale and she apologized repeatedly in both English and Spanish, quickly printing the correct label with shaking hands. By the time Omar got home to their small apartment, he had already texted his wife about the close call and had carefully set multiple alarms for his new medicine schedule—he wasn't taking any chances.

**Comprehension Questions & Answers:**

1. How long had Omar been coughing?
   - **Answer:** For three frustrating days, keeping his wife awake at night.

2. What was the pharmacist doing when Omar noticed the problem?
   - **Answer:** The tired-looking pharmacist was checking his ID.

3. How did Omar feel when he saw the wrong name?
   - **Answer:** His stomach dropped. He worried about taking the wrong medicine.

4. Why did Omar switch to Spanish?
   - **Answer:** To make sure the pharmacist understood. He wanted to communicate clearly about the mistake.

5. What had Omar done by the time he got home?
   - **Answer:** He had already texted his wife about the close call and had set multiple alarms.

6. What might have happened if Omar hadn't noticed?
   - **Answer:** He could have taken the wrong medicine, which could be dangerous.

## Round 2: Reading Scripts (Stories 3 & 4 on slides)

Use the same relay flow as Round 1: one partner reads from the script you provide, then retells; the other answers questions (slide reveals match the items below).

### Reading 3 — Jina's Story: Twisted Ankle

**Vocab bank:** stirring, kimchi stew, buzzed urgently, shaky, twisted ankle, swollen, elevated, purple bruise, sprain, tension

**Full Text:**

Jina was stirring a pot of kimchi stew when her phone buzzed urgently. Her 12-year-old son's voice was shaky—he had twisted his ankle during gym class basketball and couldn't walk. Before Jina even arrived at the school, her mind was racing with worry. The school nurse, a calm older woman, had already put a bag of ice on the swollen ankle and had elevated his foot on two pillows. At urgent care, the Korean-speaking doctor carefully examined the purple bruise and said the ankle was not broken—just a bad sprain. He needed to rest for three days, no basketball practice. Jina felt the tension release from her shoulders.

**Comprehension Questions & Answers:**

1. What was Jina doing when her phone buzzed?
   - **Answer:** She was stirring a pot of kimchi stew.

2. What had the nurse already done before Jina arrived?
   - **Answer:** She had put ice on the swollen ankle and elevated his foot on pillows.

3. What did the doctor say about the ankle?
   - **Answer:** It was not broken—just a bad sprain; rest three days, no basketball practice.

4. How did Jina feel at the end?
   - **Answer:** She felt the tension release (relief).

### Reading 4 — Marco's Story: Sleep Deprivation

**Vocab bank:** delivery driver, exhaustion, eyes burning, stumbled, route, falling asleep at the wheel, sympathetically, night feedings, partner

**Full Text:**

Marco, a delivery driver, had not slept well for a whole week—his new baby daughter was crying every two hours. He was driving his route when exhaustion hit him like a wave, his eyes burning. He pulled over safely and stumbled into a café for strong black coffee. By the time he reached his office to report the issue, he had already called his clinic for an urgent appointment, worried about falling asleep at the wheel. The nurse, who had kids of her own, listened sympathetically and suggested practical solutions: reduce late-night screen time (stop checking his phone during baby's night feedings), drink less soda after dinner, and maybe ask his partner to take one night shift so he could sleep.

**Comprehension Questions & Answers:**

1. Why hadn't Marco slept well?
   - **Answer:** His baby daughter was crying every two hours for a whole week.

2. What was Marco doing when exhaustion hit him?
   - **Answer:** He was driving his delivery route.

3. What had Marco done by the time he reached his office?
   - **Answer:** He had already called his clinic for an urgent appointment.

4. What advice did the nurse give?
   - **Answer:** Reduce screen time at night, less soda after dinner, partner takes one night shift.

## Teaching Notes

### Grammar Focus

**Past Perfect vs Past Continuous:**

- Past Perfect: actions completed BEFORE another past action  
  - "By the time her sister arrived, Lucia had already vomited"
- Past Continuous: actions in progress when something else happened  
  - "Lucia was shivering at the bus stop when she started feeling dizzy"

### Common Mistakes to Watch For

1. **Using past perfect for the LAST event** (incorrect)  
   - ❌ "She had felt relieved at the end"  
   - ✅ "She felt relieved at the end"

2. **Sequence markers:** first, then, after that, before, by the time, when, while

### Differentiation Tips

**For Lower Level Students:**

- Allow partners to look at text during retell
- Provide sentence frames: "First, ___ was ___ when..."
- Use native language discussion for comprehension check

**For Higher Level Students:**

- Remove vocab bank
- Add idiomatic expressions requirement
- Ask predictive/inferential questions only

### Timing Guidance

- Reading: 2:00 per story
- Retell prep: 1:00 (where shown on slides)
- Video: 2:00 watch + memorize (slides)
- Questions: 2–3 minutes per set
