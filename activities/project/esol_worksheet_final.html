Before your group writes

  • Discuss pros and cons for your topic.
  • Agree on a rule in full sentences (your own words — not only one missing word).
  • Be ready to say why you picked words like must, can, or cannot.

Modals: what they mean and how strong

% on the line Modal word What it means
0% (start) Very strong “__________” (serious / danger)
25% Often in class: __________ — not allowed
45% Often in class: __________ — you don’t need to
55% Often in class: __________ — it is OK / allowed
85% Often in class: __________ — the school or law says so
100% (end) Very strong "__________” (you feel it is necessary)

Example — ice cream in school (how to fill out your page)

This is a model from class. Each topic below is the same: good points and bad points, then your rule, then why you used words like must or can.

Pros (ideas for allowing) Cons (worries or problems)

It can make students happy and give them energy.

It can be a reward for good behavior.

It can be a fun treat during long school days.

It can be messy and break things at school.

It is not a healthy snack for every day.

It can make desks and floors sticky.

Our rule (write a full sentence) — example

Students can eat ice cream in the cafeteria during lunch, but they must not eat it in the classroom.

Why we used this word / these words — example

We used can because ice cream is OK sometimes as a treat. We used must not in the classroom because it is too messy by books and computers.
