Part 1: Reading Apartment Listings

Look at the apartment listings (Images 1–5). Answer the questions for each listing using complete sentences when possible.

Listing 1: Back Bay Studio
  1. What is the monthly rent?
  2. What two utilities are included in the rent?
  3. Is the washing machine and dryer in the apartment or in the building?
  4. Are pets allowed?
  5. Why do you think this apartment is a studio and not a one-bedroom?
Listing 2: South Boston
  1. How many bedrooms and bathrooms does this apartment have?
  2. What is the total size of the apartment in square feet?
  3. Is parking included in the rent?
  4. What is the apartment located close to?
  5. Which features make this apartment more expensive than Listing 1?
Listing 3: Dorchester
  1. How much is the rent per month?
  2. Are utilities included? If yes, which ones?
  3. What is one special feature of the outside area?
  4. Are pets allowed?
  5. What kind of family or person would like this apartment? Why?
Listing 4: Seaport
  1. How much is the rent per month?
  2. Does this apartment have central air conditioning?
  3. When will this apartment be available?
  4. What neighborhood is this apartment in?
  5. Which amenities make this apartment the most expensive so far?
Listing 5: East Boston
  1. How many bedrooms does this apartment have?
  2. What two transportation options is this apartment located near?
  3. Is parking included?
  4. What is the total size of the apartment?
  5. Why might this apartment be a good choice for someone without a car?

Part 3: Persona Matching

Read about each person or group. Choose the best apartment for them. Be ready to explain your answer.

A. The Student

Maria is a student who needs to save money. Her maximum budget is $2,200/month. She has no car and no pets.

Best apartment: _______

B. The Family

The Chen family has two children and a large dog. They need at least two bedrooms and a safe outdoor space. Budget: $3,600/month.

Best apartment: _______

C. The Young Professional

David works long hours downtown. He wants a luxury apartment with a gym and pool. Budget: $4,200/month; no pets.

Best apartment: _______

D. The Roommates

Sarah and Jessica need two bedrooms and parking for one car. They enjoy the beach. Budget: $3,300/month.

Best apartment: _______

E. The Minimalist

Alex travels often. He wants a small, simple apartment with many costs included. Budget: $3,000/month.

Best apartment: _______

Part 4: Discussion Questions

Discuss with a partner or small group.

  1. Which apartment is the most expensive, and why do you think it costs so much?
  2. Which apartment is the cheapest? What could be a problem with the cheapest option?
  3. Which apartment is best for someone without a car? How do you know?
  4. Imagine you are one of the people in Part 3. What is the most important thing you look for in an apartment?
  5. What does “W/D in unit” mean? Why is it better than “W/D in building”?
  6. If you were a landlord, which apartment would be the easiest to rent? The hardest? Why?

Part 5: Asking Follow-Up Questions

The 4-Level Question Strategy:

  • Initial: Ask the basic question (What? When? How much?)
  • Clarify: Get specific details ("Does that include...?" "Is that...?")
  • Dig deeper: Ask about flexibility, exceptions, options
  • Plan ahead: "What if...?" "How would...?" "What would happen if...?"
