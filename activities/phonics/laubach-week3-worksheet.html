Sit vs Seat - Short I vs Long E
Learning the Difference Between /ĭ/ and /ē/
Short I = /ĭ/ (like "sit")     Long E = /ē/ (like "seat")
In English, these sounds are different! Short I = /ĭ/ (sounds like "ih" - like in "sit", "bit", "hit") Long E = /ē/ (sounds like "ee" - like in "seat", "beat", "heat"). Practice saying "sit" (/ĭ/) and "seat" (/ē/) - hear the difference! Short I is quick, Long E is long like "ee".

Reading: "Finding a Seat"
Finding a Seat
Lisa needed to find a seat on the bus. She looked around and saw many people sitting. Some people sat in the front seats. Some people sat in the back seats. Lisa walked between the rows of seats. She looked for an empty seat. Finally, she found a seat near the window. Lisa sat down in the seat. She was happy to sit and rest her feet. The bus was very hot. The heat from the sun made Lisa feel warm. She was glad to sit in a seat with a window. The cool air from the window helped beat the heat.
While Lisa sat in her seat, she thought about her day. She had to meet her friend at three o'clock. She had to complete her work before she could meet her friend. Lisa looked at her watch. It was almost time to meet her friend. The bus moved slowly. Lisa hoped she would not be late. She wanted to meet her friend on time. Lisa sat quietly in her seat. She watched the people around her. Some people sat and read books. Some people sat and looked at their phones. Everyone had a seat to sit in.
When the bus stopped, Lisa got up from her seat. She walked to the door. Lisa stepped off the bus with both feet on the ground. She was happy to meet her friend. Her friend was waiting for her. They decided to sit together at a café. They found two seats at a small table. Lisa and her friend sat down. They talked and laughed. They had a good time together. Lisa was glad she found a seat on the bus so she could meet her friend on time. It was a complete and happy day.
