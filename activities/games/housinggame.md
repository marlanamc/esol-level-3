# Housing Game — All Questions

24 discussion and role-play prompts, grouped by category.

---

## Daily Life at Home

### 1. Favorite Room
- Ask everyone in your group: "What is your favorite room in your home and why?"
- Then share about your own favorite room. Describe at least three things in the room.

### 2. Weeknight at Home
- Describe a typical weeknight at home in Boston. What time do you get home, and what do you usually do?
- Then ask a partner questions about their weeknight at home.

### 3. Room Tour
- Pretend you are making a short video tour for a friend in another country. Describe one room in your home.
- Use *there is* / *there are*, and name at least five objects in the room.

### 4. Household Chores
- Explain which chores you do at home and how often you do them. Example: "I take out the trash on Sunday."
- Ask each player one question about chores in their home.

### 5. First Home in Boston
- Talk about the first place you lived in Boston or in the U.S. Was it small or big, quiet or noisy, expensive or cheap?
- Share one thing you liked and one thing you did not like.

### 6. Dream Apartment
- Describe your dream apartment or house. Where is it, how many rooms does it have, and what is around it?
- Use at least three sentences with "I would like to have..."

---

## Landlords & Communication

### 7. Call Your Landlord: Heat Problem
- **Pick someone in your group to be the landlord.** Role-play a phone call to your landlord. The heat in your apartment is not working, and it is very cold.
- Give your name and address, explain the problem, and ask when someone can come to fix it.

### 8. Call Your Landlord: Noisy Neighbors
- **Pick someone in your group to be the building manager.** Role-play calling your building manager about very noisy neighbors at night.
- Describe the problem and ask what they can do. Use polite language like "Could you..." or "Is it possible to..."

### 9. Text Message to Landlord
- What is a text message you could send to your landlord about a small problem in your apartment?
- For example: a broken light, a loose door handle, or a leaking faucet. Include a greeting and a thank you. Group can discuss the message and reply as the landlord.

### 10. Rent Question
- **Pick someone in your group to be the landlord.** You received a letter or email about a rent increase. Role-play a conversation with your landlord.
- Ask the landlord to explain the reason for the increase and when it will start. Ask them to consider a lower increase or a discount.

### 11. New Lease
- **Pick someone in your group to be the landlord.** You want to stay in your apartment for another year, but only at your current rent price.
- Practice asking your landlord about a new lease. Ask them to consider discounts for early payments or other incentives.

### 12. Voicemail Practice
- Pretend you are leaving a voicemail for your landlord. Introduce yourself, explain one problem, and say your phone number slowly.
- Say the message out loud to your group. They will tell you if it is clear.

## Housing Problems & Solutions

### 13. Leaking Ceiling
- Your ceiling is leaking water when it rains. Explain the problem to your group in detail.
- Then, together, decide what you should do first, second, and third.

### 14. Pests in the Apartment
- You see mice or bugs in your kitchen. Talk about what you can do yourself and what the landlord should do.
- Use at least three sentences with "should" for advice (example: "The landlord should call the exterminator to fix the problem.")

### 15. Broken Elevator
- You live on the tenth floor of your building. The elevator is broken again. Explain how this affects your daily life.
- Then give two solutions you want to ask for, for example signs, repairs, or a discount.

### 16. Roommate Conflict
- **Pick someone in your group to be your roommate.** You and your roommate disagree about cleaning. She says you should clean the bathroom every day, but you think cleaning it once a week is enough.
- Role-play a calm conversation where you try to find a solution. Find a compromise that works for both of you.

### 17. Late Rent
- **Pick someone in your group to be the landlord.** Your company messed up your paycheck and you will have to pay rent a few days late this month.
- Practice what you would say to your landlord. Explain the situation politely, say when you can pay, and apologize for any inconvenience.

### 18. Laundry Problems
- The washing machine in your building is constantly broken and someone always takes your clothes out early.
- Discuss three possible rules or solutions for shared laundry spaces.

---

## Safety & Emergencies at Home

### 19. Smell of Gas
- You smell gas in your kitchen. Explain what you would do and what you would not do.
- Use examples of actions you would take, for example turn off the gas, open the window, call the gas company, and leave the house.

### 20. Fire Alarm
- You are at home and the fire alarm starts to ring. Describe what you should do step by step.
- Then ask your group: "Where is the exit in your building?" and "Do you know your building's rules for fire?"

### 21. Water from Upstairs
- **Pick someone in your group to be the landlord.** Water is coming through your ceiling from the apartment upstairs.
- Describe how you feel, what you say to your neighbor, and what you tell your landlord.

### 22. Stranger at the Door
- **Pick someone in your group to be the stranger.** Someone you do not know is knocking on your apartment door late at night.
- Discuss what you would do. Then role-play a short conversation through the closed door.

### 23. No Heat in Winter
- **Pick someone in your group to be the landlord.** It is a very cold January day in Boston, and your apartment has no heat.
- Talk about temporary solutions inside your home, and then practice asking your landlord for urgent help.

### 24. Calling 911 from Home
- Choose one emergency at home, for example a fire, someone falling down the stairs, or smoke in the hallway.
- Role-play a call to 911. Give your address, explain the emergency, and answer two questions from the operator.
